Rift guide

Accessibility

The screen reader

Press Super, Alt and S to turn the screen reader on, and the same keys to turn it off. It is Orca, the screen reader GNOME uses, and it speaks with a voice that is on the drive, so it needs no network.

Orca reads the window you are in, the row you are on and the text you type, and it has keys of its own: hold Insert and press H to hear what each key does, and Insert and space for its settings. The Applications menu has a row called Screen reader that does the same as the keys.

A screen reader can only read an app that says what it is showing. The apps in the image do; an app you install may not.

The on-screen keyboard

Press Super, Alt and K to show the on-screen keyboard, and the same keys to hide it. It takes the bottom of the screen, the windows above it make room for it, and what you press on it goes to the window you were in, the same as a key on a real keyboard. There is a row in the Applications menu for it as well.

Text and colours

The desktop is dark by default. A light desktop, and the rest of what the look is made of, is a setting that comes with the Settings app.

The interface font is Noto Sans at eleven points, and apps follow it.
